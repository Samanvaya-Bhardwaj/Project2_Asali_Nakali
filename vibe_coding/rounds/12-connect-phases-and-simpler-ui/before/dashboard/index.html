A counterfeit pack looks fine.

The WHO estimates that one in ten medical products in developing nations is substandard or falsified. The evidence is rarely in any single system. It shows up as a disagreement between them: a serial no manufacturer ever issued, a temperature log that ruins a biologic, one barcode scanned in two cities ten minutes apart.

Asali / Nakali queries five of those systems at once and reconciles what they say against each other.

The problem

Why this can't just be one database

Every obvious fix runs into the same four obstacles, and they are the reason the project is built as a mediator rather than a warehouse.

1

The holders are rivals

Private pharmacies, logistics firms and regulators have no reason to merge their records into one system, and good reasons not to.

2

The formats differ

JSON documents in one place, normalised SQL in another, a master registry in a third and a REST service in the fourth.

3

The keys don't match

One physical pack is scan_code at the counter, batch_serial in transit, product_uid in the registry and item_ref in a complaint.

4

Some sources outrank others

A statutory recall is not one opinion among four to be averaged. It has to override everything else, so the system needs two tiers of arbitration rather than one score.

How it works

What happens when you scan a pack

A global-as-view mediator rewrites one question into each silo's own dialect, collects the answers, and then decides what they add up to.

Step 1

Read the serial, then resolve it

Scanners misread characters and staff sometimes type a batch number instead of a product code. Three string metrics (Jaro-Winkler, Levenshtein and Soundex) decide which known pack the input actually refers to before any lookup happens.

Step 2

Split the question four ways

The mediator turns one query into four native sub-queries: a document lookup, two SQL joins and an authenticated REST call. They run against both nodes in parallel, and each answer is normalised into a shared vocabulary on the way back.

Step 3

Arbitrate, then record the decision

A veto gate runs first: recalls, blacklists and unregistered serials force the score to zero on their own. Anything that survives goes to weighted consensus, and the result is appended to a hash chain so it can be audited later.

Coursework

The integration problems underneath

Eight classical problems in information integration, each one wired to something you can operate in the explorer rather than only read about.

1

Query unfolding

One global-as-view query is rewritten into four native dialects and the results recomposed into a single dossier.

2

Structural flattening

Nested facility trees and location hierarchies are collapsed into the flat rows the mediator compares on.

3

Units and semantics

Temperatures in Fahrenheit or Kelvin, prices in dollars, timestamps as epoch milliseconds. All converted before anything is compared.

4

Entity resolution

Three string metrics vote on whether a mangled scan refers to a known pack, and the reasoning is shown rather than hidden.

5

Conflict arbitration

Written policies decide which silo wins a disagreement: statutory authority, physical feasibility, and caution about patient safety.

6

Partition tolerance

Cut the regulatory node off and a circuit breaker trips. The mediator answers from what remains and caps its own confidence.

7

Schema evolution

A silo upgrades to GS1 digital links and minor-unit currency while running. The adapter absorbs the change without a redeploy.

8

Identity across disjoint keys

No two silos share a primary key. Bridge keys close the graph from batch number to product code to complaint reference, so one pack stays one entity.
